# Kiyosi Itô

**Hook:** He started out at Japan's Cabinet Statistics Bureau during the war, and gave the world a chain rule for random paths. Wall Street, physics, and biology still use it.

## Known for

- **Stochastic calculus:** integrals and differential equations driven by random noise such as [[Glossary#Brownian motion|Brownian motion]]. His foundational papers appeared in the 1940s. See [[Stochastic Calculus]].
- **Itô's formula:** the chain rule with an extra term, needed because random paths are too rough for the ordinary one.
- **Influence:** the mathematics behind the [[Black-Scholes Equation]] and much of modern probability.

## Life in brief

- **1915:** born in Mie Prefecture, Japan. Graduated from the Imperial University of Tokyo in 1938.
- **1939-43:** Cabinet Statistics Bureau. His 1942 paper on random processes, a foundation of stochastic integration, dates from this time.
- **1952-79:** professor at **Kyoto University**, including a term as director of its Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences. Also held posts at Aarhus and Cornell.
- **Prizes:** Wolf Prize (1987), Kyoto Prize (1998), and the first **Gauss Prize** for applications of mathematics (2006).
- **2008:** died in Kyoto, aged 93.
