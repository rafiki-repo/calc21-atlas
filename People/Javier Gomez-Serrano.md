# Javier Gómez-Serrano

**Hook:** A fluids analyst who trusts computers to help *prove* theorems, not just to make pictures. He is at the center of two big AI-for-math projects: neural networks that hunt for fluid blow-ups, and DeepMind's AlphaEvolve. "There are far easier ways to make a million dollars," he told Brown University.

## Known for

- **Computer-assisted proofs:** his 2013 Madrid PhD, supervised by [[Cordoba and Martinez-Zoroa|Diego Córdoba]], was titled *Analytical and Computer-assisted proofs in incompressible fluids*. Rigorous computation, where the computer's rounding errors are tracked and bounded, has stayed at the core of his work.
- **Splash singularities:** with Castro, Córdoba, Fefferman, and Gancedo, he showed that a water wave can curl over and hit itself in finite time (*PNAS*, 2012; *Annals of Mathematics*, 2013).
- **Neural-network blow-up hunt:** he joined [[Tristan Buckmaster]] and Ching-Yao Lai in 2022. Their Sep 2025 preprint with Google DeepMind reports new *unstable* **[[Glossary#Singularity|singularities]]** in fluid equations related to Navier-Stokes. The team calls the solutions possible starting points for computer-assisted proofs, and none is proved yet. Brown reports the method is being pointed at simpler 1D and 2D models first.
- **AlphaEvolve at scale:** with Bogdan Georgiev, [[Terence Tao]], and Adam Zsolt Wagner, he tested DeepMind's code-evolving system on 67 problems. Brown reports a *PNAS* paper is forthcoming. See [[AI as Research Collaborator]].

## Career

Trained in both mathematics and telecommunications engineering in Barcelona, then took his master's and PhD at the Universidad Autónoma de Madrid. He was an instructor and assistant professor at Princeton and a distinguished researcher at the University of Barcelona. He is now a professor of mathematics at **Brown University**. His homepage lists research interests from fluid mechanics to spectral geometry and machine learning.
