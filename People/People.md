# People

Voices worth knowing: the people who built calculus, and the people shaping how it is researched, taught, and explained today. Back to [[Home]].

Each person note says **what they contributed or are known for**, with sources, and describes living people factually.

## Forerunners
- Archimedes (c. 287-212 BCE): found areas and volumes by slicing and balancing. See [[Archimedes Palimpsest]]
- Madhava of Sangamagrama (c. 1340-1425): infinite series for π, sine, and cosine. See [[Kerala School]]

## Founders
- Isaac Newton and Gottfried Leibniz: see [[Newton vs Leibniz]]

## Builders of the toolkit
- [[Kiyosi Ito|Kiyosi Itô]] (1915–2008): founder of stochastic calculus, first Gauss Prize winner (2006)
- Abraham Robinson: made infinitesimals rigorous in the 1960s. See [[Nonstandard Analysis]]

## Today's voices
### Researchers
- [[Terence Tao]]: Fields Medal analyst, textbook author, and a central figure in AI-assisted mathematics
- [[Hong Wang]]: 2026 Fields Medalist; proved the 3D Kakeya conjecture with Joshua Zahl
- [[Yu Deng]]: 2026 Fields Medalist; derives fluid and kinetic equations from particle dynamics
- [[Cordoba and Martinez-Zoroa|Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa]]: the Madrid duo whose blow-up blueprint the 2026 AI fluid results built on
- [[Levent Alpoge|Levent Alpöge]]: number theorist behind the AI-assisted 2026 Jacobian counterexample

### Educators and communicators
- [[Grant Sanderson]]: creator of 3Blue1Brown and its *Essence of Calculus* series
- Steven Strogatz: author of *Infinite Powers* (2019), a history of calculus for general readers. See [[Resources#Books for general readers]]

### Explainers from the past
- Silvanus P. Thompson (1851-1916): physicist and author of the 1910 classic [[Calculus Made Easy]]
