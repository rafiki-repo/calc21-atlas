# Tristan Buckmaster

**Hook:** He made his name showing that fluid equations can be *too flexible*, with rough solutions that refuse to be unique. Then he went hunting for the opposite failure: smooth fluids that blow up. Since 2021 he has done much of that hunting with neural networks.

## Known for

- **Wild weak solutions:** with Vlad Vicol, a proof that **[[Glossary#Weak solution|weak solutions]]** of the Navier-Stokes equations need not be unique (*Annals of Mathematics*, 2019). Buckmaster and Vicol shared the **2019 Clay Research Award** with Philip Isett. Clay says the pair showed such solutions can be "remarkably wild." The technique, **convex integration**, grew out of his 2014 Leipzig PhD on Onsager's conjecture about turbulent energy loss.
- **Neural networks that find blow-ups:** in 2021, with Ching-Yao Lai, he began training **physics-informed neural networks** to look for **[[Glossary#Singularity|singularities]]**. By 2022 the method had rediscovered a known blow-up in the Euler equations. A Sep 2025 preprint with Google DeepMind and [[Javier Gomez-Serrano|Javier Gómez-Serrano]] reports new families of *unstable* singularities in three related fluid equations. Quanta (Jan 2026) says the approximations got about a billion times more accurate along the way. None of these candidates has been proved yet.
- **Forced Euler blow-up (Sep 2026):** with [[Levent Alpoge|Levent Alpöge]], an AI-assisted, Lean-checked claim that the forced Euler equations can blow up. It was announced a day before OpenAI's forced Navier-Stokes claim. **Claimed, not yet peer reviewed.** See [[Navier-Stokes Smoothness Problem]].
- **Also:** founding PI of the Simons Foundation collaboration on **wave turbulence**, and imploding solutions for *compressible* fluids.

## Career

Studied in Australia (Monash, ANU), then Bonn and Leipzig. He was a Courant Instructor at NYU, an assistant professor at Princeton (2017-22), and a professor at Maryland. Since 2023 he has been a professor at NYU's **Courant Institute**.
