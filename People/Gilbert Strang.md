# Gilbert Strang

**Hook:** The MIT professor who put his lectures online in 2001, before that was normal, and then gave away a full calculus textbook too.

## Known for

- **An early OpenCourseWare adopter.** He first posted his classes in 2001, the year MIT OpenCourseWare launched. MIT News reports his OCW courses have drawn **more than 20 million views**. "OCW transformed my life," he told MIT News.
- ***Calculus*** (first printed 1991, Wellesley-Cambridge Press): a full single- and multivariable textbook, now **free on OCW** as an updated 3rd edition. It runs from a "Highlights of Calculus" chapter 0 to vector calculus and a closing chapter called "Mathematics after Calculus." An instructor's manual and a study guide are also posted.
- ***Highlights of Calculus*** (OCW, 2010): **17 short videos**, five on the big ideas and twelve on derivatives, for high school students, college students, and anyone else who needs help with calculus. The theme is that one function is the derivative of the other, and the integral goes back.
- **OpenStax Calculus:** a senior contributor to the free three-volume set (see [[Free Calculus Textbooks]]).
- **Linear algebra, mostly.** Most of his books and his famous 18.06 lectures are on linear algebra, and his MIT page notes that many universities use those textbooks. It sits right next to calculus: linear algebra is the language of multivariable calculus and of [[Backpropagation and the Chain Rule|machine learning]].
- **Retired in 2023** at 88. He gave his last 18.06 lecture on **May 15, 2023**, after 61 years on the MIT faculty, and got a standing ovation. MIT News reports that the video passed 352,000 views in the following week. He planned to keep running Wellesley-Cambridge Press, which has published his books since 1986.
