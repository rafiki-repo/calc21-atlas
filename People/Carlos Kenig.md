# Carlos Kenig

**Hook:** Fluid flow, light pulses in optical fibers, and medical imaging all lead to the same kind of hard equations. Carlos Kenig has spent decades building the toolkit for them, and in 2026 it won him the King Faisal Prize.

## Known for

- **Nonlinear dispersive equations:** [[Glossary#Partial differential equation (PDE)|PDEs]] whose waves spread out as they travel, unless the nonlinearity pulls them together (see [[Glossary#Dispersive equation]]). He has worked on them for about 30 years, according to the King Faisal Prize.
- **Harmonic analysis applied to PDEs:** bringing the tools of [[Glossary#Fourier analysis|Fourier analysis]] to bear on differential equations. The King Faisal Prize describes this as the thread running through his work.
- **Large solutions over long times:** his recent work asks what large solutions to "critical" nonlinear wave equations do in the long run.

The King Faisal Prize citation (Jan 2026) is "for his contributions to the theory of nonlinear partial differential equations." It says his methods are widely used, with applications in fluid mechanics, optical fibers, and medical imaging.

## Career in brief

- **Born** in Buenos Aires (1953). BSc, University of Buenos Aires; PhD, University of Chicago (1978).
- **Princeton, then Minnesota, then back to Chicago (1985)**, where he is Louis Block Distinguished Service Professor of Mathematics.
- **President of the International Mathematical Union**, 2019-2022, which organizes the International Congress and awards the Fields Medal. Past vice-president of the AMS.
- **Honors include:** Salem Prize (1984), AMS Bôcher Prize (2008), Solomon Lefschetz Medal (2021), ICMAM Latin America Prize (2024), King Faisal Prize in Science, Mathematics (2026).
