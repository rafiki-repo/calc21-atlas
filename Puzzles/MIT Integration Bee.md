# MIT Integration Bee

**Hook:** A spelling bee, except the words are integrals. You get a few minutes each, a live audience, and the title **Grand Integrator** for whoever is left standing.

## What it is

A yearly competition held each January during MIT's Independent Activities Period and run by MIT math graduate students. It is open to MIT students only, though anyone can watch.

- **Qualifier:** 20 integrals in 20 minutes. The top 16 advance.
- **The Bee:** a "regular season" in which four students race on each integral, then an 8-player single-elimination playoff. Integrals get harder each round, and in the finals each one has a 5-minute limit.
- **2026 (Jan 21):** Grand Integrator **Karthik Vedula**. The other finalists were Tristan Chaang, Gurkirat Singh, and Jacopo Rizzo.

## A taste (2026 finals, Problem 1)

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{nx^2+kx+n}\,dx = \frac{5\pi^2}{72}$$

The first step is spotting the Riemann sum hiding inside: as n grows, the sum becomes a second integral.

## Why people love it

Textbook integrals are built to be solvable. Bee integrals are built to be solvable *fast* by someone who spots the trick, such as a symmetry, a substitution, or [[Feynman Integration Trick|differentiating under the integral sign]]. Many universities and schools now run their own bees.
