# Basel Problem

**Hook:** Add up 1 + 1/4 + 1/9 + 1/16 + ... forever. The total is finite, and it is exactly π²/6, a number that comes from circles.

## The problem

What is the sum of the reciprocals of the perfect squares, 1/n² for n = 1, 2, 3, ...?

- **Posed:** by Pietro Mengoli in **1650**. Members of the Bernoulli family tried it and failed. It is named after **Basel**, their hometown and Euler's.
- **Solved:** by Leonhard Euler, age 28, in **1734**. His answer was π²/6 ≈ 1.644934, and it was read at the St. Petersburg Academy in December 1735.
- **Made rigorous:** Euler's first argument treated sin x like an infinitely long polynomial, a step nobody could justify at the time. He gave an accepted proof in **1741**.

## Why people love it

- Nothing about the sum mentions circles, yet π appears.
- It is a doorway: the sum is ζ(2), the first interesting value of the **Riemann zeta function**. Euler went on to find ζ at every even integer. The odd values are much harder: see [[Irrationality of Zeta Values]].
- It has dozens of proofs, using calculus, Fourier series, complex analysis, and even plain geometry.
