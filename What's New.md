# What's New

Latest additions and changes to the Atlas, newest first. Back to [[Home]].

## 2026-11-20
- **Two open problems on the move.** [[Irrationality of Zeta Values]]: a Sep 2026 preprint by Aabir Fauzan claims that ζ(5) = 1 + 1/2⁵ + 1/3⁵ + ... is irrational. That would be the first named odd zeta value since Apéry's ζ(3) in 1978. Lean and Isabelle versions already check it, and peer review has not happened yet. Euler's constant γ is still a mystery. The [[Moving Sofa Problem]]: Jineon Baek's 119-page proof that Gerver's 1992 sofa is the largest that fits around a corner, uses Green's theorem and no computer. It is reported to be under review at *Annals*.
- **New person:** [[Steven Strogatz]], author of *Infinite Powers* and host of *The Joy of Why*.
- **Updated:** [[Research Watch]] (two new rows), [[Proof Assistants and Lean]] (the ζ(5) formalization), [[Basel Problem]] (what happens at odd powers). The [[Glossary]] adds *Euler's constant* and *irrational number*. Trails 2 and 3 in [[Rabbit Holes]] get new stops.

## 2026-11-19
- **Calculus in public.** Two new notes on the culture around calculus. [[Calculus Made Easy]] is Silvanus Thompson's 1910 book for the terrified, still in print and free online. The [[Calculus or Statistics Debate]] covers whether high school calculus is the ticket to college: a Texas study found similar long-term earnings, Caltech changed its rules in 2023, and the evidence is still thin.
- **The people behind the 2026 shocks.** New profiles of [[Levent Alpoge|Levent Alpöge]] (the Jacobian counterexample, announced in a one-line post) and of [[Cordoba and Martinez-Zoroa|Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa]], whose blow-up blueprint the AI fluid results built on. [[Jacobian Conjecture]], [[Navier-Stokes Smoothness Problem]], [[AI as Research Collaborator]], and [[Research Watch]] now link to them.
- **New shelf:** "Books for general readers" in [[Resources]], including Steven Strogatz's *Infinite Powers*.
- **New trails:** "Calculus in public" and "The people behind the 2026 shocks" in [[Rabbit Holes]].

## 2026-11-18
- **Calculus before calculus.** Two new [[Stories]]: the [[Archimedes Palimpsest]] (Archimedes' slicing method survives in one copy, under a prayer book; a lost page turned up in Blois, France, in March 2026), and the [[Kerala School]] (Madhava's series for π, sine, and cosine around 1400, an 1834 paper that was ignored, and a Europe-transmission idea that has no documentary evidence so far).
- **New person:** [[Kiyosi Ito|Kiyosi Itô]], who built [[Stochastic Calculus]].
- **Navier-Stokes update:** [[Navier-Stokes Smoothness Problem]] adds *Scientific American*'s report on the forced-vs-unforced question ("The most important problem is unsolved," says Luis Silvestre).
- **Fixed:** [[Black-Scholes Equation]] now gives Fischer Black's death (1995) and why he could not share the 1997 prize.
- **New trail:** "Calculus before calculus" in [[Rabbit Holes]]. The [[Glossary]] adds *method of exhaustion* and *power series*.

## 2026-11-17
- **Strange calculi arrive.** Three new [[Frontiers]] notes: [[Fractional Calculus]] (half derivatives, and why they remember the past), [[Stochastic Calculus]] (Kiyosi Itô's calculus for random paths), and [[Nonstandard Analysis]] (infinitesimals made rigorous, with a free textbook built on them).
- **New application:** the [[Black-Scholes Equation]] for pricing options, which turns into the heat equation.
- **Navier-Stokes update:** [[Navier-Stokes Smoothness Problem]] now quotes Clay's Sep 11 statement (it reports that the problem "has apparently been settled" and calls its review "deliberately unhurried") and an Oct 2026 preprint arguing that OpenAI's Lean proof and written proof don't match. The problem is still listed as active.
- **New trail:** "Strange calculi" in [[Rabbit Holes]]. The [[Glossary]] adds *Brownian motion* and *fractional derivative*.

## 2026-11-15

[[AI as Research Collaborator]] and [[Research Watch]] are updated to match.
- **Puzzles grows up.** New: the [[MIT Integration Bee]] (2026 Grand Integrator: Karthik Vedula), the [[Putnam Competition]] (including the 2025 AI entrant), and the [[Feynman Integration Trick]]. [[Basel Problem]] is now source-checked, with a link to a geometric video proof.

## 2026-11-14

Meet [[Hong Wang]] and the needle-turning [[Kakeya Conjecture]] (3D proof posted in 2025 with Joshua Zahl), and [[Yu Deng]], whose work on [[Hilbert Sixth Problem|Hilbert's sixth problem]] aims to derive fluid equations from colliding particles (a preprint, still labeled as a claim).
- **New map:** [[Research Watch]] tracks recent results and prizes in analysis, each tagged preprint, claimed, or published.
- **Updated:** [[Navier-Stokes Smoothness Problem]] now explains how it differs from Hilbert's sixth problem. The [[Glossary]] adds *Fourier analysis*, *Hausdorff dimension*, and *partial differential equation*.
