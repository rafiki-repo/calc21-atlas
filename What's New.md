# What's New

Latest additions and changes to the Atlas, newest first. Back to [[Home]].

## 2026-11-17
- **Strange calculi arrive.** Three new [[Frontiers]] notes: [[Fractional Calculus]] (half derivatives, and why they remember the past), [[Stochastic Calculus]] (Kiyosi Itô's calculus for random paths), and [[Nonstandard Analysis]] (infinitesimals made rigorous, with a free textbook built on them).
- **New application:** the [[Black-Scholes Equation]] for pricing options, which turns into the heat equation.
- **Navier-Stokes update:** [[Navier-Stokes Smoothness Problem]] now quotes Clay's Sep 11 statement (it reports that the problem "has apparently been settled" and calls its review "deliberately unhurried") and an Oct 2026 preprint arguing that OpenAI's Lean proof and written proof don't match. The problem is still listed as active.
- **New trail:** "Strange calculi" in [[Rabbit Holes]]. The [[Glossary]] adds *Brownian motion* and *fractional derivative*.

## 2026-11-16
- **Applications opens.** The section's first three notes: [[Backpropagation and the Chain Rule]] (how neural networks learn), the [[SIR Epidemic Model]] (thresholds, final size, and herd immunity), and [[Weather Forecasting]] (from Richardson's 1922 hand calculation to ECMWF's machine-learning model, which has run alongside the physics model since Feb 2025).
- **First educator profile:** [[Grant Sanderson]] of 3Blue1Brown. The [[Resources]] page gets a videos shelf.
- **Source-checked:** [[Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]] (exact statements and conditions, plus who found it) and [[Newton vs Leibniz]] (a dated timeline, including the Royal Society report Newton wrote himself). All five seed notes are now checked.
- **New trail:** "Calculus at work" in [[Rabbit Holes]]. The [[Glossary]] adds *chain rule* and *gradient descent*.

## 2026-11-15
- **Status corrections.** Two open-problem notes were out of date. [[Jacobian Conjecture]]: an AI-assisted counterexample disproved it in 3 and more dimensions (July 2026). The 2D case is still open. [[Navier-Stokes Smoothness Problem]]: OpenAI claims a Lean-checked proof of forced blow-up (Sep 2026). It is still under review, and Clay lists the problem as "active". [[AI as Research Collaborator]] and [[Research Watch]] are updated to match.
- **Puzzles grows up.** New: the [[MIT Integration Bee]] (2026 Grand Integrator: Karthik Vedula), the [[Putnam Competition]] (including the 2025 AI entrant), and the [[Feynman Integration Trick]]. [[Basel Problem]] is now source-checked, with a link to a geometric video proof.
- **New trail:** "Integrals for sport" in [[Rabbit Holes]]. The [[Glossary]] adds *counterexample*, *Jacobian*, and *Riemann sum*.

## 2026-11-14
- **The 2026 Fields Medals, through a calculus lens.** Two of the four medals went to analysts. Meet [[Hong Wang]] and the needle-turning [[Kakeya Conjecture]] (3D proof posted in 2025 with Joshua Zahl), and [[Yu Deng]], whose work on [[Hilbert Sixth Problem|Hilbert's sixth problem]] aims to derive fluid equations from colliding particles (a preprint, still labeled as a claim).
- **New map:** [[Research Watch]] tracks recent results and prizes in analysis, each tagged preprint, claimed, or published.
- **Updated:** [[Navier-Stokes Smoothness Problem]] now explains how it differs from Hilbert's sixth problem. The [[Glossary]] adds *Fourier analysis*, *Hausdorff dimension*, and *partial differential equation*.

## 2026-11-13
- **Learn it free.** The [[Resources]] section gets its first real shelf: [[Free Calculus Textbooks]] (OpenStax vs Active Calculus), [[MIT OCW Single Variable Calculus]], and the 3D visualizer [[CalcPlot3D]].
- **New in Education:** [[Widening Access to Calculus]] maps 2026 efforts (Learnvia, MIT4America, Khan Academy) and notes what has not been measured yet. The [[Glossary]] adds *gradient* and *vector field*.
- **Calculus meets AI.** A new map, [[Calculus and AI]], covers where chatbots stumble, how proof checkers work, and how AI is entering research. It comes with two companion notes: [[Proof Assistants and Lean]] and [[AI as Research Collaborator]].
- **New person:** [[Terence Tao]]. **New resource:** [[Lean Companion to Analysis I]], a calculus course a computer can check.
- **Updated:** [[Navier-Stokes Smoothness Problem]] now covers the AI-assisted blow-up work on related fluid equations, which does not solve the problem. The [[Glossary]] adds *autoformalization*, *formal proof*, and *proof assistant*.
- **Atlas created.** Initial structure, section hubs, a starter [[Glossary]], and a handful of seed notes: [[Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]], [[Newton vs Leibniz]], [[Basel Problem]], [[Navier-Stokes Smoothness Problem]], [[Jacobian Conjecture]]. Seed notes are marked as needing a source check.
