# Jacobian Conjecture

**Hook:** A question about polynomial maps that fits on a napkin and has resisted proof since 1939.

## The question

Take a polynomial map from n-dimensional space to itself. Its **Jacobian determinant** measures, at each point, how the map stretches or squashes volume. The conjecture: if that determinant is a nonzero *constant* everywhere, then the map must have a polynomial inverse.

## Status

Described in the literature as open for general dimension (it is known in dimension 1). **Needs a source check and a look for recent claims.** Past claimed proofs have been found to contain errors, so treat any new claim with care until it is confirmed.

## Why it connects to calculus

The Jacobian is the multivariable version of the derivative, and the conjecture asks when a local "invertibility" signal becomes a global one.
