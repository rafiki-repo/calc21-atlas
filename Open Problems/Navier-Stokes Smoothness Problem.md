# Navier-Stokes Smoothness Problem

**Hook:** The equations describing how water and air flow are over a century old and used daily, yet nobody has proved that their solutions always behave sensibly.

## The question

The Navier-Stokes equations model fluid motion. In three dimensions, can a smooth starting flow develop a singularity (a point where quantities blow up) in finite time, or do solutions always stay smooth? Proving either answer is one of the Clay Mathematics Institute's Millennium Prize Problems.

## Why it connects to calculus

It is a question about **differential equations** and about what "smooth" (differentiable, repeatedly) really guarantees.
