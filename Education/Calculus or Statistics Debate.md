# Calculus or Statistics Debate

**Hook:** For decades, high school calculus has been the unofficial ticket to a selective college. Should it be? A long-running US argument now has some data, plus a famous school that changed its rules.

## The argument in one line each

- **For keeping calculus central:** it is the gateway to STEM majors, and the alternatives may turn into lower tracks under a new name.
- **For more pathways:** most college students never need calculus, many high schools do not offer it, and statistics or data science may suit non-STEM students better.

## What the evidence says (so far)

**The Texas study** (Thomas B. Fordham Institute and UT Austin; reported by Education Week, Apr 2025) compared matched groups of students who took **AP Calculus AB** or **AP Statistics**, 2015-2020:

- Calculus takers were **more likely** to enroll at the most selective colleges (+6 points), choose a STEM major (+11), and finish a STEM degree (+10).
- Both groups were **equally likely** to complete a bachelor's degree.
- **Long-term earnings were about the same.** The differences were not statistically significant.
- Co-author Adam Tyner reads this as calculus *sorting* students toward STEM rather than *causing* higher pay. That is the authors' interpretation of one state's data.

**The admissions signal:** Tyner cites a survey in which 74% of 133 admissions officers said AP Calculus is among the math classes that "carry the most weight" (in an opinion piece for The 74, Oct 2025).

## Where policy moved

- **Caltech (Aug 2023)** dropped calculus, physics, and chemistry as *required high school courses*. Applicants still have to show mastery, and one accepted route is free online courses from Khan Academy. Its admissions director put the new question as: "Can you showcase to us that you have proficiency and mastery?"
- **Others (Nov 2023):** a survey by The 74 of 20 competitive schools found only **Cornell** still required calculus. Six recommended or encouraged it (MIT expects two semesters but lets students place out).
- **University of California (Jul 2023):** a faculty committee debated whether high school **data science** courses could stand in for Algebra II. Its minutes record concerns that the courses were not rigorous enough, and the committee reversed course. Strictly, that fight was about the road *to* calculus, not calculus itself.

## What is not known

- Whether new data science courses are as rigorous as AP Statistics. The Texas study looked only at AP.
- Whether the results from Texas hold in other states.
