# Calculus Made Easy

**Hook:** A 1910 book whose first chapter is titled "To Deliver You From The Preliminary Terrors", and whose motto is "What one fool can do, another can." More than a century later, it is still in print and free to read online.

## What it is

- **Author:** Silvanus P. Thompson (1851-1916), a British physicist and engineer. Some printings of the 1914 second edition were signed only "F.R.S." (Fellow of the Royal Society), without his name.
- **The pitch:** calculus for people scared of it. Thompson treats *dx* as "a little bit of x" and builds the rules of [[Glossary#Derivative|differentiation]] and [[Glossary#Integral|integration]] from there. He skips formal [[Glossary#Limit|limits]] almost entirely.
- **Staying power:** the first edition was reprinted four times and the second fifteen times. In 1998 **Martin Gardner** published a revised edition (St. Martin's Press). It adds chapters on functions, limits, and derivatives, plus recreational problems.

## Good for / not good for

- **Good for:** a gentle, funny first pass at derivatives and integrals; adults returning to the subject; a look at how calculus was explained before the modern textbook.
- **Not good for:** rigor. Its informal "little bits" are close in spirit to the infinitesimals that [[Nonstandard Analysis]] later made precise, but the book does not justify them. Notation and examples are Edwardian.

## Access

- [calculusmadeeasy.org](https://calculusmadeeasy.org/): a free HTML edition, converted by hand from the Project Gutenberg text (ebook #33283). Checked 2026-11-19. The site also sells an optional solutions guide and a print copy.
- The original text is public domain in the US. Gardner's 1998 revision is a separate, copyrighted book.
