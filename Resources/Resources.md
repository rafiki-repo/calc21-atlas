# Resources

The best places to learn, explore, and compute. Back to [[Home]].

**Each resource note lists:** what it is, who it's for, cost or login requirements, and the date the link was last checked.

## Textbooks

- [[Free Calculus Textbooks]]: OpenStax and Active Calculus compared (free, open-licensed)
- Keisler's *Elementary Calculus: An Infinitesimal Approach*: first-year calculus done with infinitesimals instead of limits (free online). See [[Nonstandard Analysis]].

## Books for general readers

- [[Calculus Made Easy]]: Silvanus Thompson's 1910 antidote to "preliminary terrors" (free, public domain in the US)
- Steven Strogatz, *Infinite Powers* (2019): calculus told as a story, from ancient Greece to gravitational waves (commercial; [author's page](https://www.stevenstrogatz.com/books/infinite-powers))

## Courses and lectures

- [[MIT OCW Single Variable Calculus]]: a full MIT semester built for self-study (free)
- MIT 18.642 stochastic calculus lectures: Itô's formula through Black-Scholes (free, advanced). See [[Stochastic Calculus]].

## Visualizations and interactive tools

- [[CalcPlot3D]]: spin surfaces and vector fields in the browser (free)

## Videos and channels

- 3Blue1Brown: visual explainers, including *Essence of Calculus* (free). See [[Grant Sanderson]].

## Rigorous and formal

- [[Lean Companion to Analysis I]]: an undergraduate analysis course written in machine-checkable form (free)

## Problem archives

- [[Putnam Competition]]: links to Kiran Kedlaya's free archive of Putnam problems and solutions, 1985-2025
- [[MIT Integration Bee]]: links to past bee integrals with answers (free)

## Elsewhere in the Atlas

- [[Widening Access to Calculus]]: free courseware, tutoring programs, and AI tutors
