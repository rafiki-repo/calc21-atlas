# Newton vs Leibniz

**Hook:** Two brilliant people developed calculus around the same time, and the argument over who deserved the credit soured relations between British and continental mathematicians for generations.

## The short version

- **Isaac Newton** developed his "method of fluxions" in the 1660s, but published little of it for years.
- **Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz** developed his own version in the 1670s and published first, in the 1680s.
- Newton's supporters later accused Leibniz of borrowing from Newton's unpublished work. The Royal Society, then led by Newton, issued a report backing Newton's priority. Historians generally note that this was not an impartial process.
- The modern consensus, as commonly stated, is that both arrived at the ideas independently.

## Why it still matters

- **Notation:** Leibniz's notation (such as *dy/dx* and the integral sign ∫) is what most of the world uses today.
- **Culture:** The dispute is often cited as a cautionary tale about priority, publication, and national pride in science.

## Worth knowing

Both built on earlier work by others, and neither made calculus fully rigorous. That took until the 19th century.
